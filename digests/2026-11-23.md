## Canada

### [Saskatchewan Introduces Prevention of Deceptive Artificial Intelligence Act](https://docs.legassembly.sk.ca/legdocs/Bills/30L3S/Bill30-76.pdf)
*Medium importance · 2026-11-17*

**What happened:** Saskatchewan Justice Minister Tim McLeod introduced Bill 76, The Prevention of Deceptive Artificial Intelligence Act, in the provincial legislature. The proposed law establishes civil causes of action and allows the Court of King's Bench to order the removal and destruction of deceptive synthetic media and award damages to victims.

**Why it matters:** The legislation is Canada's first comprehensive provincial civil framework targeting malicious deepfakes and AI disinformation, creating direct legal liability for bad actors and introducing mandatory AI disclosure rules in key sectors.

### [Canada Establishes Advisory Council on Artificial Intelligence and Culture](https://www.canada.ca/en/canadian-heritage/news/2026/11/ministers-miller-and-solomon-announce-members-of-the-advisory-council-on-artificial-intelligence-and-culture.html)
*Medium importance · 2026-11-19*

**What happened:** Canadian Identity and Culture Minister Marc Miller and Artificial Intelligence Minister Evan Solomon announced the membership of the newly created Advisory Council on Artificial Intelligence and Culture and held its inaugural meeting. The council brings together creators, technology experts, and industry leaders to advise on national AI policy and the creative sector.

**Why it matters:** The council will guide the Canadian government on copyright reform, AI governance, and cultural protections under Canada's National AI Strategy, shaping how generative AI tools interact with copyrighted creative works.

### [Treasury Board Reports on Consultations for Federal AI Register](https://www.canada.ca/en/government/system/digital-government/digital-government-innovations/responsible-use-ai/advancing-ai/progress.html)
*Low importance · 2026-11-18*

**What happened:** The Treasury Board of Canada Secretariat published a What We Heard report detailing stakeholder and public feedback on its prototype Government of Canada AI Register. The feedback will inform the development of a permanent, mandatory registry of AI systems across federal institutions.

**Why it matters:** Federal departments and public sector vendors will face updated transparency standards, enhanced documentation rules, and recurring reporting requirements for public-facing automated decision systems.

## United States

### [Commerce Department Restricts Remote Foreign Access to Advanced Semiconductors](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/19/2026-23529/addressing-remote-access-to-advanced-semiconductors)
*High importance · 2026-11-19*

**What happened:** The Commerce Department Bureau of Industry and Security issued an interim final rule establishing new restrictions to prevent foreign entities in Country Group D:5 and Macau from remotely accessing advanced computing power based on U.S. technology. The rule restricts exports, reexports, or in-country transfers to overseas data centers that supply significant compute volumes to these entities.

**Why it matters:** The regulation closes a loophole allowing foreign companies to bypass hardware export controls by leasing remote cloud infrastructure. It directly affects cloud service providers, overseas data center operators, and developers training advanced AI models.

### [ACUS Solicits Feedback on Generative AI in Agency Adjudication](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/19/2026-23539/policies-on-the-use-of-generative-super-intelligence-in-agency-adjudication-request-for-comments)
*Medium importance · 2026-11-19*

**What happened:** The Administrative Conference of the United States issued a request for public comments on developing policies regarding the use of generative AI by agency adjudicators, personnel, and outside parties. The input will support an ongoing ACUS project to recommend best practices across the federal government.

**Why it matters:** The initiative will establish federal standards for how administrative judges, agency officials, and litigants can use generative tools in formal proceedings while preserving evidentiary standards and procedural fairness.

### [House Bill Proposes CISA Lead Oversight of Federal AI Agents](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/11076)
*Low importance · 2026-11-18*

**What happened:** House lawmakers introduced HR 11076 to amend the Homeland Security Act of 2002. The bill directs the Director of the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency to lead federal cybersecurity efforts regarding the adoption, monitoring, authorization, and risk management of AI agents operating on executive agency networks.

**Why it matters:** The measure would create targeted federal oversight and security guidelines specifically focused on autonomous AI agents operating within government IT infrastructure.

### [Senate Bill Introduced Mandating SEC Rules on Corporate AI Use](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/5785)
*Low importance · 2026-11-19 · Financial services*

**What happened:** Lawmakers introduced S 5785, directing the Securities and Exchange Commission to issue rules governing the use of artificial intelligence by public companies. The legislation was read twice and referred to the Senate Committee on Banking, Housing, and Urban Affairs.

**Why it matters:** If enacted, the bill would require the SEC to establish binding disclosure and risk governance requirements for public companies and reporting financial institutions utilizing AI tools.

## European Union

### [ECB Warns of Financial Stability Risks from AI Valuations and Infrastructure Debt](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2026/html/ecb.pr261118~83c5c60158.en.html)
*Medium importance · 2026-11-18 · Financial services*

**What happened:** The European Central Bank published its semi-annual Financial Stability Review, warning that high valuations for AI companies and growing reliance on debt to finance AI infrastructure create systemic vulnerabilities. The ECB cautioned that an equity repricing shock could spill into credit and bond markets.

**Why it matters:** The warning puts euro area banks and non-bank lenders on notice regarding their direct and indirect credit exposures to tech hardware, cloud infrastructure, and private credit financing for AI assets.

### [European AI Board Adopts New Rules of Procedure and Reviews National Enforcement](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/ai-board-holds-its-tenth-meeting)
*Medium importance · 2026-11-20*

**What happened:** The European AI Board convened its tenth meeting, chaired by the Irish EU Council Presidency, and formally endorsed new Rules of Procedure adopting an elected two-year chairmanship model. Participants reviewed member state progress on implementing the AI Act, including national market surveillance authority appointments, regulatory sandboxes, and prohibited practices.

**Why it matters:** The updated governance rules formalize how national regulators will coordinate enforcement actions and align regulatory sandboxes across the EU as the AI Act implementation schedule advances.

### [AI Act Advisory Forum Convenes Second Plenary and Elects Co-Chairs](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/ai-act-advisory-forum-holds-its-second-meeting)
*Low importance · 2026-11-20*

**What happened:** The AI Act Advisory Forum held its second meeting, electing its two co-chairs representing commercial and civil society stakeholders and adopting its formal Rules of Procedure. The Forum initiated stakeholder discussions on potential workstreams requested by the AI Office, focusing on AI companions and AI agents.

**Why it matters:** The operational framework allows external civil society and industry representatives to advise the European Commission and the European AI Board on emerging risks and enforcement practices.
