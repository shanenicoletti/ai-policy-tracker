*Medium importance · 2026-11-17*

**What happened:** Saskatchewan Minister of Justice and Attorney General Tim McLeod introduced Bill No. 76, The Prevention of Deceptive Artificial Intelligence Act, in the provincial Legislative Assembly. The bill establishes civil remedies against malicious AI-generated deepfakes, grants courts the authority to mandate content takedowns and award damages, and updates provincial election and consumer protection rules to mandate AI disclosures.

**Why it matters:** Establishes Canada's first comprehensive provincial legislative framework specifically targeting deceptive synthetic media, imposing civil liabilities on creators and distributors of malicious AI content and setting new transparency requirements.

## United States

### [New York City Council Approves Landmark Ban on Algorithmic Surveillance Pricing](https://council.nyc.gov/press/2026/11/12/3339)
*High importance · 2026-11-12 · Financial services*

**What happened:** The New York City Council passed Introduction 891-A, landmark legislation sponsored by Speaker Julie Menin that bans 'surveillance pricing' across the city. The bill makes it unlawful for any entity or service provider to use algorithms and consumer personal data to charge different prices to different customers for the same goods or services.

**Why it matters:** Creates strict consumer protection compliance obligations for retailers, e-commerce platforms, delivery apps, and ride-sharing services operating in NYC by eliminating personalized algorithmic price differentiation and establishing a significant municipal model for automated pricing governance.

### [US Commerce Department Issues Rule Restricting Remote Access to Advanced AI Semiconductors](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-23529.pdf)
*High importance · 2026-11-16*

**What happened:** The Department of Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) issued an interim final rule (RIN 0694-AK50) titled 'Addressing Remote Access to Advanced Semiconductors.' The regulation establishes export license requirements to prevent entities headquartered in arms-embargoed jurisdictions (Country Group D:5, including China) or Macau from remotely accessing advanced AI computing power through global third-party data centers.

**Why it matters:** Directly impacts global cloud infrastructure providers, semiconductor exporters, and data centers by closing pathways for foreign restricted entities to train or fine-tune frontier AI models using overseas advanced compute.

*Medium importance · 2026-11-12 · Financial services*

**What happened:** The European Commission formally published interpretive guidelines under Article 96(1)(d) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (the AI Act) regarding compliance with the Article 50 transparency obligations for AI systems. The guidance clarifies requirements for AI interaction notices (such as customer chatbots), synthetic media marking, emotion recognition/biometric categorisation disclosures, and deepfake labelling.

**Why it matters:** This provides binding national market surveillance authorities and private deployers—including financial institutions using customer-facing chatbots or synthetic content—with explicit compliance benchmarks ahead of statutory enforcement deadlines.
